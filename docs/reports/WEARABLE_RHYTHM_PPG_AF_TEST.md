# Wearable ML — rhythm head on real PPG with AF labels (external test)

Generated by `python -m src.ml.wearable.serving.external_rhythm` → `data/processed/wearable/external/mimic_perform_af.json`. External test only: no model was retrained or changed.

## Data

**MIMIC PERform AF Dataset** (Charlton et al., Physiol. Meas. 2022; Zenodo [10.5281/zenodo.6807403](https://zenodo.org/records/6807403), ODbL 1.0, derived from the MIMIC-III Waveform Database).
- 35 ICU adults: 19 in AF, 16 not in AF. 20 min each.
- Fingertip PPG and lead-II ECG from the bedside monitor, both at 125 Hz.
- The labels are per subject, so every window inherits its subject's label.
- Downloaded as the two WFDB zips (11.9 + 10.2 MB, MD5 verified). They stay in `_zips/` and are read in place by `load_mimic_perform_af()`.
- None of these patients is in the rhythm training data (MIT-BIH, AFDB, LTAFDB).

## Method

- **Head:** registry rhythm head v2.0.0 (1D-CNN on RR intervals). It was trained on ECG RR with PPG-like augmentation.
- **Settings:** the same as in serving. Windows are 60 s (40 frames), one window every 4 frames, and a window is scored only when `head_available` allows it. The registry threshold is 0.61.
- **Two inputs, each run through the emulator → `FeatureEngine` replay:**
  - **(a) PPG:** the PPG is resampled to 64 Hz (the E4 rate used in training). Beats come from `emulator.detect_beats`, then the firmware RR window is applied. This is the device path.
  - **(b) ECG:** R-peaks come from `data/ecg.detect_rpeaks`, the validated detector. This is the reference. The gap between (a) and (b) is the cost of PPG beat detection.
- **Subject-level score:** the median window probability for each subject.
- **95 % CIs:** 1,000 bootstrap resamples of subjects, stratified by class.

Per-subject notes (medians are in the JSON):
- **non_af_012** is the only subject-level PPG error, a false positive (median p = 0.70 on PPG, 0.01 on ECG). On PPG only 1,100 RR intervals were accepted, against 1,633 on ECG. The missed beats look like irregular rhythm.
- **non_af_014** is borderline on PPG (median p = 0.32, against 0.04 on ECG).
- **af_008** is the only subject-level ECG miss (median p = 0.41 on ECG, 0.81 on PPG).
- **af_004:** the ECG detector found too few beats (77 % of windows available). This is the only ECG availability loss.

## Interpretation

The head carries over from ECG RR to real PPG RR. On PPG, window AUROC is 0.99 and macro-F1 is 0.94 at the unchanged 0.61 threshold. Every subject is ranked correctly by its median probability.

The (a)/(b) comparison separates the two sources of error:
- **The model:** on clean RR it separates AF from non-AF almost perfectly (ECG window AUROC 1.00). Its errors at 0.61 are AF windows called regular, so the threshold is slightly conservative here.
- **PPG beat detection:** missed or extra beats add irregularity. This costs specificity: 0.94 on PPG against 1.00 on ECG, and one false-positive subject. A beat-quality gate, for example from the HR-trust head, would target exactly this.

## Limitations

- **Fingertip, not wrist:** ICU fingertip PPG from a bedside monitor is cleaner than wrist PPG from the MAX30102. There is little motion, and the contact and perfusion are different. Wrist performance, especially in motion, will be lower. This test does not measure it.
- **Small sample:** n = 35 subjects. The CIs are wide, and subject-level AUROC = 1.0 cannot be told apart from roughly 0.95.
- **Per-subject labels:** AF can be paroxysmal and "non-AF" can include ectopy, so window labels are noisy. The ICU population (sicker, older, on vasoactive drugs) is not our target population.
- **Different task:** the head was trained for regular vs any abnormal rhythm (AF, flutter, other annotated rhythms). Here it is scored only on AF vs non-AF.
- **Sampling rate:** the PPG was resampled from 125 to 64 Hz to match training. The device's own beat detector is firmware, not this emulator.
